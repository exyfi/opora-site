Adult ADHD self-check: 18 questions from the WHO scale

Researchers at the World Health Organization and Harvard built the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS v1.1) from the 18 ADHD symptoms in the DSM. Answer for the past 6 months. You need about 5 minutes.

The scale screens for ADHD. Only a clinician can diagnose it or name its type. Your answers stay in this browser: Google Analytics gets the start, the finish and the result group, and nothing else.

Hyperactivity and impulsivity 0 of 9

The mark at 5 shows the DSM-5 symptom count for adults.

Pattern: combined

Both groups reach five. The DSM-5 calls this the combined presentation: attention slips, and restlessness and quick actions show up too.

Pattern: mostly inattentive

Inattention reaches five and hyperactivity stays below it. The DSM-5 calls this the predominantly inattentive presentation, once known as ADD. You lose focus, miss details and misplace things, while outward restlessness stays low.

Pattern: mostly hyperactive-impulsive

Hyperactivity reaches five and inattention stays below it. The DSM-5 calls this the predominantly hyperactive-impulsive presentation: restlessness, talking over people, trouble waiting.

Pattern: neither group reaches five

Neither count reaches the DSM-5 line for adults. Sleep, anxiety, depression and stress can also break focus, and a clinician can tell these apart from ADHD.

A DSM-5 type also needs symptoms before age 12, in at least two settings such as work and home, and a clear cost to your life. A self-report can’t check any of that.

How the scoring works

Each question has a cut-off. Questions 1, 2, 3, 9, 12, 16 and 18 count from Sometimes. The other eleven count from Often.

counted

The screener result uses Part A only: four or more counted answers out of six match the pattern the WHO scoring looks for. The profile uses all 18 and splits them into the two DSM-5 groups, nine questions each.

Three presentations of ADHD

  • Inattentive

    Focus drifts, details slip, things go missing. From the outside it can look like daydreaming.

  • Hyperactive-impulsive

    A restless body, fast talk, trouble waiting. In adults the motor often moves inside and shows up as inner restlessness.

  • Combined

    Both groups of symptoms at the same time.

Questions and scoring: Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS v1.1) Symptom Checklist, World Health Organization. The symptom groups follow the DSM-5. The 5 of 9 mark is the DSM-5 adult threshold and sits outside the WHO scoring.
