ADHD task paralysis: how to start when you can’t

You know the task, you may even want it done, and you still sit there for an hour. Many adults with ADHD call this task paralysis. Doctors don’t use the phrase as a diagnosis.

What tends to cause it

ADHD affects executive functions: the skills you use to plan, start, and switch between tasks. A few situations make the freeze more likely.

  • You face too many tasks with no order among them, so you can’t pick the first one.
  • The task is so big that you can’t see its first move.
  • The reward sits far away, and the task feels boring right now. Piers Steel’s procrastination equation describes this: motivation drops as the delay grows.
  • You expect to fail, and each stalled hour makes the next start harder.

Six ways to get moving

  1. Shrink the first step to under two minutes. “Call the dentist” stalls. “Find the number” you can do now.
  2. Start a 5-minute round. Promise yourself you can stop when it ends.
  3. Cut the urgent list to three. Pick three tasks at most for today and move the rest out of sight.
  4. Split the elephant. Write the big task as its pieces, and treat only the first piece as today’s job.
  5. Calm your body first. If the freeze comes with panic, try 5-4-3-2-1 grounding or paced breathing for a minute, then go back to the small step.
  6. Pick tomorrow’s step tonight. Morning-you skips the deciding and does the step.

If the freeze lasts for weeks and spreads to your whole life, talk to a doctor. Low mood, anxiety and poor sleep can look like task paralysis.
